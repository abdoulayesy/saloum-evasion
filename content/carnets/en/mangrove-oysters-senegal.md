In the Saloum Delta, oysters do not grow in farms: they cling to the aerial roots of the mangroves, covered and uncovered by the tide every day.

## An oyster born from the mangrove

At low tide, mangrove roots appear covered in small oysters fed by the delta’s brackish water [to check with the team].

## A harvest by women

In most villages, women harvest the oysters by pirogue or on foot in the mud [to check], following the tides.

## From harvest to plate

The oysters are mostly grilled over a wood fire, where they open by themselves, and served with lemon, chilli or onion sauce [to check].

## Where to taste them

During a meal with a local family when in season, or after a pirogue trip through the bolongs.

## Joining a harvest

It is sometimes possible to join the harvesters, with their consent and the village’s [to check: conditions and season]. Ask us before your stay.
